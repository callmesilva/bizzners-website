## Intent

The disqualification-killer. Bizzners' realistic prospect is a small or mid-sized producer
who has quietly decided that exporting is for bigger companies than theirs. This film is
aimed squarely at that belief.

Source copy:

- `experience.intro` — *"The size of your company doesn't matter. If you have expandable
  productive capacity — or want to widen your commercial reach into other markets — we can
  power your growth... It's about clearing the 'out of reach' barrier."*
- `growth.heading` — *"Growing is not just a matter of size"*
- `growth.p` — *"...companies that are not merely interested in your product — they have
  the solidity, capacity, structure and market position to give you the commercial push you
  need."*
- `closing.cta` — *"Let's talk about your next market."*

**The reversal is the film.** It spends 14 seconds establishing a genuinely small operation
— and then reveals that the size that actually matters is the *buyer's*, not the seller's.
That turn is why the film works on someone who thinks they're too small: it agrees with
them about scale, then relocates it.

Scene 2 shows two candidate buyers being **rejected** before the third is accepted. Showing
the rejections is what makes the selection claim credible. A film that only shows the good
warehouse is a film that shows nothing.

## SCENE 1 — Small · 0:00–0:14

**Location** `PLANT`, then `BAY`.
**Actors** `PRODUCER`, `WORKER`, and one unnamed third worker — **three people, total, in
the entire operation.** That headcount is the whole point of the scene and every prompt
enforces it.
**Focus** Close and affectionate. This scene likes these people. Nothing here is shot to
look pitiable or struggling — small is not the same as failing, and if the grade or the
blocking suggest otherwise the film loses the audience it was written for.
**Camera** Intimate, handheld micro-movement, slightly closer than comfortable.
**Palette** Warm neutrals, daylight-led. The warmest scene in the film.
**Transition IN** From black.
**Transition OUT** Hard cut on the beat of silence before V3.

## SCENE 2 — Selection · 0:14–0:30

**Location** Three different `WAREHOUSE` candidates. The first two are deliberately *not*
the Location Bible warehouse — they are lesser versions of it, and each is described from
scratch.
**Actors** None identifiable. This scene is about places, not people. Human presence is
distant and incidental in all three.
**Focus** Deep focus, wide lenses, all three shot from the same position and height — the
identical camera setup is what makes them read as candidates being compared rather than
three unrelated locations. **Match the setup exactly across 2-A, 2-B and 2-C.**
**Camera** Identical slow push forward down a central aisle in each. Same speed, same
lens, same height. Only the space changes.
**Palette** 2-A is drained and cool. 2-B is harsh and yellow-green. 2-C is the brand's own
blue-white. The grade does the judging.
**Transition IN** Hard cut into the film's turn.
**Transition OUT** Straight cut into 3-A, which continues 2-C's push forward — same space,
same move, no perceptible break.

## Assembly notes

**The film turns at 0:14** and everything is built to make that turn land: the beat of
silence from 0:12.5, the cut from the warmest scene to the coolest, and V3 arriving after
the picture has already changed. Cut the picture first, then drop the VO in 1.0s late. The
delay is what makes it feel like a thought rather than a caption.

**Scene 2's three shots must share one camera setup.** Same lens, same height, same push
speed, same duration feel. Generate all three in a single batch if the model supports it. If
the setups drift, the audience reads three warehouses; if they match, the audience reads a
selection process — which is `cooperation.pillars[5].gloss`, *"Partners and terms measured
before commitment,"* delivered without a single word.

**Do not make small look sad.** The most likely way this film fails is a grade or a
performance that pities the producer in Scene 1. He is not struggling; he is capped. The
brief for Scene 1 is *competence with a ceiling*. If a take reads as defeat, discard it —
the audience for this film is the man in that shot, and he will not sit through 45 seconds
of being pitied.

**Music map.**
- 0:00 — small, intimate, single instrument
- 0:12.5 — drops to almost nothing
- 0:14 — cold and thin through both rejections
- 0:25 — opens on the accepted warehouse
- 0:35 — full and orchestral
- 0:41 — resolve
- 0:42.5 — endcard over the tail

**9:16 crop.** This film ships vertical for paid and outreach, so the crop is a primary
deliverable rather than an afterthought. Two shots need real work:

- **1-A** is a wide that exists to show an entire small room. At 9:16 it loses that. Crop
  centre on the production line and accept the reduced read — or, better, generate a
  dedicated 9:16 pass of this one prompt with the framing re-composed vertically.
- **2-A / 2-B / 2-C** are central-aisle pushes, which is the single most crop-friendly
  composition there is. The vanishing point sits centre-frame in all three and survives
  vertical intact. This is why the scene was blocked that way.

Keep subtitles inside the middle 60% of frame height so platform chrome never covers them,
and keep the last shot's faced cans above the subtitle band.
