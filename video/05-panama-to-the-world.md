## Intent

The short one. It is the film that gets sent, not the film that gets found — attached to a
WhatsApp message, dropped in a DM, run as a cold-audience ad.

It compresses film #1's argument into six shots and one idea: **what you make can be
somewhere else.** Origin → gateway → world, with the endcard carrying the only call to
action in the slate that a viewer can act on with one thumb.

Source copy (staged, never spoken):

- `hero.kicker` — *"International trade facilitation · Panamá"*
- `chain.line1/line2` — *"The link in the chain / that decides your success."*
- `contact.whatsappLabel` — *"Chat on WhatsApp"*, the endcard's only instruction.

**It must land silent.** Feed autoplay is muted and most viewers never unmute. Every beat
is carried by image alone. The music is a bonus for the minority who turn it on, and it is
never load-bearing.

## SCENE 1 — Origin · 0:00–0:06

**Location** `PLANT`, then `BAY`.
**Actors** `PRODUCER` in 1-B, hand and forearm only. No faces anywhere in this film — at
2.5-second shot lengths a face cannot be read, and a half-read face is a distraction.
**Focus** Macro and shallow. Vertical framing wants a single subject stacked in the centre
of the frame; there is no room for a second point of interest.
**Camera** Tight, minimal movement. Fast cuts plus a moving camera reads as chaos on a
phone.
**Palette** Cool steel with one warm daylight note.
**Transition IN** No fade. Hard in on motion.
**Transition OUT** Hard cut.

### Shot 1-A · 0:00–0:03.5 · 3.5s

```

conveyor inside a small, clean light-industrial canning plant, the line running from the
bottom of the frame toward the top, pale concrete floor and exposed steel roof trusses

windows. The cans catch the light in a steady repeating rhythm. No people. 50mm, shallow
depth of field, focus on the cans in the centre of the vertical frame. Locked off. The
conveyor is already in motion at the first frame.
```

`+ SHOT NEGATIVE: readable can labels, branding, static opening frame, fade in`

**Sound** Conveyor, cans touching. In from frame one, no ramp.

## SCENE 2 — Gateway · 0:06–0:12

**Location** `LOCKS`, then macro.
**Actors** None.
**Focus** 2-A is the widest shot in the film and the only one that has to carry geography.
2-B is the tightest. The jump between them is the scene.
**Camera** Locked off, both.
**Palette** Blue hour. This is the brand navy, on screen, for three straight seconds — the
most `#232d7d` frame in the slate.
**Transition IN** Hard cut.
**Transition OUT** Hard cut on the seal of the link.

### Shot 2-A · 0:06–0:09.0 · 3.0s

```
GEN: Vertical composition. The lock chamber of the Panama Canal at blue hour, enormous
steel lock gates parting on a vertical seam directly in the centre of the vertical frame,
dark green water below, low concrete walls, mooring locomotives on the rails, tropical
humidity in the air, deep navy-blue sky filling the upper half of the frame. The gates open
slowly and symmetrically, the vertical seam widening through the whole shot to reveal open
water beyond. No people. 24mm, locked off low near the water line, deep focus, fully

```
`+ STYLE (§1)` `+ NEGATIVE (§2)`
`+ SHOT NEGATIVE: tourists, viewing platform, boats in the chamber, daylight, drone shot`

**Sound** Deep water, a low ship's horn.
**Note** The Canal's vertical gate seam is the single luckiest composition in this slate —
it is *already* 9:16. Put it dead centre and let the frame do the work.

### Shot 2-B · 0:09.0–0:12.0 · 3.0s

```
GEN: Vertical composition. Extreme close-up macro of a single heavy galvanised steel chain
link taking load, the link rotating slightly and drawing tight against its neighbour, metal
surface scratched and worn with fine texture visible, cold steel-blue rim light from a hard
source at the left, deep shadow behind, dark background with terminal lights far out of

centre of frame. Locked off. The link tightens and holds.
```
`+ STYLE (§1)` `+ NEGATIVE (§2)`

**Sound** One low metallic groan under tension.
**Note** This is `chain` — *"The link in the chain that decides your success."* The same

recognise the shot.

## ENDCARD · 0:17.0–0:20.0 · 3.0s

Per `00-style-bible.md` §7, **9:16 variant**:

```
Background   solid #232d7d
Wordmark     "bizzners®" — the "r" in #6c9bff, rest #ffffff
Tagline      "Business Builders", letter-spaced ~0.34em, #ffffff at 70%
Contact      "bizzners.com   ·   +507 6000-4345"
CTA          WhatsApp glyph + "Chat on WhatsApp"
```

Three seconds, not the standard 2.5 — feed viewers scroll faster but read slower, and this
is the only endcard in the slate that is asking for an action rather than signing a film.

**Keep the entire lockup inside the middle 60% of frame height.** Platform chrome eats the
top and bottom of a 9:16 feed video, and the CTA is the one element that cannot afford to
be covered. The `contact.whatsappHref` in `src/content/site.ts` already carries a prefilled
message — *"Hello Bizzners — I'd like to talk about taking my product to new markets."* —
so use that same link wherever the ad's tap target is configured. The film and the landing
behaviour should say the same thing.

## Assembly notes

**Generate vertical. Never crop.** A 16:9 shot centre-cropped to 9:16 throws away 56% of
the frame and every one of these compositions was built for the vertical aspect — the
conveyor runs bottom-to-top, the Canal gates part on a centred vertical seam, the chain runs
top-to-bottom, the ship sits low with a tall sky. Cropping film #1's masters would produce
six mediocre shots instead of six good ones. The one exception is the 1:1 secondary
deliverable, which crops cleanly from this 9:16 master because everything is centred.

**Cut rhythm accelerates.** 3.5 / 2.5 / 3.0 / 3.0 / 2.5 / 2.5. Six shots in seventeen
seconds, tightening toward the endcard. Nothing lingers, because nothing can.

**Music:** one 17-second build, no vocal, resolving under 3-B and carrying clean through
the endcard. Deliver at −14 LUFS.

**Watch it muted before signing it off.** Twice. Then watch it on a phone at arm's length,
which is the only viewing condition that matters for this film. If the product isn't legible
as *a thing that got made and then went somewhere*, the film has failed regardless of how
good the frames look on a monitor.

**The 1:1 secondary** crops from the centre of this master with no recomposition needed. It
exists for feeds that letterbox vertical video; if you are not running those placements,
skip it.
