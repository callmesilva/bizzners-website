## Intent

The flagship. It makes the emotional argument that the rest of the slate then explains.

It is the only film that has to work with **no voice and no text at all**, because it
autoplays muted under the site hero. So the imagery isn't decoration here — it *is* the
argument, structured as three acts: a good product with nowhere to go → the link that
carries it → the market opening.

Source copy it dramatises (nothing is spoken; these are the ideas being staged):

- `hero.titleA/titleB` — *"Your business, projected — one step away."*
- `chain.line1/line2` — *"The link in the chain / that decides your success."*
- `growth.p` — *"Stepping into the international market takes the right buyer or redistributor."*

**The through-line is a threshold.** A closed door, then a door that opens, then a step
taken across it. "One step away" is staged literally, and it is the only thing the viewer
needs to read to get the film.

## SCENE 1 — The Ceiling · 0:00–0:22

**Location** `PLANT`, then `BAY`.
**Actors** `WORKER` (1-B), `PRODUCER` (1-C). Both are competent and unhurried. Neither is
frustrated — this is important. The film is not about failure, it is about a ceiling. They
are doing excellent work in a space that is too small for it.
**Focus** Long lenses, shallow. The product is always sharp; the room falls away. The
message of the focus is *the product is ready even if the world hasn't seen it.*
**Camera** Slow, deliberate, mechanical. Dollies and pushes only — no handheld chaos.
**Palette** Cool concrete grey and steel, one warm amber note from the daylight. Desaturated.
**Transition IN** Hard cut from black.
**Transition OUT** Match cut on a horizontal line — 1-D ends on the dark bar of the shut
shutter; 2-A opens on that same bar beginning to rise.

## SCENE 2 — The Link · 0:22–0:48

**Location** `BAY` → `LOCKS` → `ROOM` → `TERMINAL` → macro.
**Actors** `PRODUCER` and `BUYER` in 2-C, hands only, no faces. This is the one place in
the film where two parties meet, and the film refuses to make it about personalities —
which is precisely the brand's position (*"an organizational concept very different from a
sales department"*).
**Focus** The scene rack-focuses outward. It begins tight on a mechanism and ends tight on
a mechanism, but between them it opens to the widest imagery in the film.
**Camera** Motion inherits: every shot in this scene has something *parting, rising or
tightening*. The eye is being taught that a channel is opening.
**Palette** The navy arrives here. Blue hour, sodium amber against `#232d7d` sky. This is
the most on-brand scene in the slate.
**Transition IN** Match cut on the horizontal line of the shutter.
**Transition OUT** Hard cut on the music's first downbeat into 3-A.

## Assembly notes

**Cut rhythm.** Scene 1 is slow and even (7/6/5/4) — it *decelerates* into the shut door.
Scene 2 holds steady. Scene 3 opens with the longest shot in the film (7.0s) and then
tightens. The film breathes out, holds, breathes in.

**Music map.**
- 0:00 — piano alone, sparse, unresolved
- 0:18 — everything thins out to a single held note under the shut door
- 0:22 — the shutter sound punches through; low synth pad enters
- 0:34 — cello enters over the table
- 0:48 — first percussion, on the cut to open water
- 1:06 — resolve
- 1:12.5 — cut to endcard, music continues clean underneath and tails out

**The two deliverables of this film.**

1. **Master, 75.0s, with endcard.** LinkedIn, deck, anywhere the film is watched.
2. **Hero loop cut, 72.5s, no endcard, muted.** For the website. Drop the endcard and cut
   the last frame of 3-D directly back to the first frame of 1-A. Both are centred
   rectangles of light against dark surroundings; the loop reads as the light going out
   and another producer's door coming into view. The loop point is invisible if 3-D's
   white-out is graded to match 1-A's clerestory highlights — grade the two together.

**Never let this film depend on sound.** Watch the whole cut muted before signing it off.
If any beat is unreadable without audio, it is broken — it will be watched muted far more
often than not.
